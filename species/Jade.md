## Resume light watering as growth starts
- id: co1
- category: other
- start: 04-15
- end: 05-15

Water a little more often as new growth appears, but only once the compost has dried out — wrinkled leaves mean it's thirsty, not a fixed schedule.

## Repot in fast-draining mix (every 2–3 years)
- id: co2
- category: repot
- start: 05-01
- end: 05-25

Use a fast-draining, gritty/inorganic mix — jade likes to be slightly under-potted, not root-bound. Don't water for about a week afterwards, so cut roots can dry and callous; watering too soon leads straight to root rot.

## Start monthly balanced feed
- id: co3
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 09-01

A balanced feed once a month from mid-spring to autumn is all it needs — strong light matters far more than fertiliser. Stop feeding once autumn sets in.

## Structural pruning & trunk work
- id: co4
- category: prune
- start: 05-15
- end: 06-20

Jade tolerates hard cuts and trunk chops well and responds by budding freely, so prune regularly to force branching — especially low on the trunk, where it builds the tree. Never seal cuts: a jade's limbs are heavy with stored water and cut paste traps moisture against the wound, inviting rot. Leave it open to dry.

## Move outdoors after the last frost
- id: co5
- category: other
- start: 06-10
- end: 06-25

Once nights are reliably frost-free, move to a bright, sheltered spot outdoors — acclimate gradually over 1–2 weeks to avoid sunburn, and keep it out of prolonged rain.

## Wiring — withhold water first
- id: co6
- category: wire
- start: 06-15
- end: 07-31

Wire loosely on lignified branches and bend gradually — the soft bark marks easily and branches can snap. Don't water beforehand; turgid branches are far more likely to snap and mark. Branches set in 3–4 weeks — check weekly, as wire bites in fast.

## Pinch new growth through summer
- id: co7
- category: prune
- start: 06-01
- end: 09-15

Continually pinch new shoots back to 2–3 leaf pairs to build ramification and keep leaves small. Stop by mid-September so late growth has time to harden before coming back indoors.

## Peak summer watering
- id: co8
- category: other
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-31

Water most often now, but never on a schedule: let the compost dry between waterings, which pushes the roots to go looking for moisture, and water the moment it has dried out. Wrinkled leaves mean underwatered; mushy or yellowing leaves mean overwatered. Red tips or edges on the leaves are a good sign — that's the tree telling you it's getting enough light.

## Take cuttings
- id: co9
- category: propagate
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-15

Stem or leaf cuttings root readily in sandy, well-drained compost — even a single leaf laid on the surface will strike. Let cut ends callous for a few days first for best results.

## Bring indoors — inspect for pests first
- id: co10
- category: pest
- start: 08-25
- end: 09-15

Bring it back indoors well before the weather turns, checking closely for mealybug, scale and spider mites before it comes inside — infestations are more likely in winter's warm, dry indoor air. Treat with isopropyl alcohol, insecticidal soap or neem. If overnight temperatures approach 5°C outdoors, bring it in immediately regardless of date.

## Taper watering and stop feeding
- id: co11
- category: other
- start: 09-01
- end: 10-15

Ease off watering and stop feeding as growth slows for the year — indoors, this is the point to switch to a bright, cool spot.

## Winter rest — keep above 5–7°C, water sparingly
- id: co12
- category: other
- start: 10-15
- end: 04-10

Never let it drop below about 5–7°C — this is a frost-tender succulent, not a hardy bonsai. Water as little as once every 2–3 weeks, only when leaves start to wrinkle; cold, wet roots are the main way jade dies over winter. It needs no true dormancy, but it does appreciate this resting period on reduced water. Clusters of small starry white-to-pink flowers may appear given enough light — sources put them anywhere from late summer to spring and agree they're rare on an indoor plant, so treat any bloom as a bonus rather than something to chase.
