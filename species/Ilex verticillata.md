## Repot (every 2 years) as buds extend
- id: il1
- category: repot
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-15

Repot in spring as new buds extend, in a basic free-draining mix, root-pruning as you go. Every second year is the gentler of the two bonsai cadences on offer, and it fits PFAF's warning that this species resents root disturbance — increasingly so as it ages. Ease off further on an old tree rather than keeping to a schedule.

## Fortnightly feeding
- id: il2
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 09-01

Feed every two weeks right through the growing season.

## Flowering — keep a pollinator nearby
- id: il3
- category: other
- start: 06-01
- end: 06-30

Winterberry is dioecious: a male plant must be near a female for berries to set. The small flowers open in early summer, so keep both sexes together while pollinators are active.

## Trim to shape
- id: il4
- category: prune
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-15

Trim new growth to shape through the growing season. To keep the autumn berry display, avoid removing this year's fruiting wood.

## Pest watch
- id: il5
- category: pest
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-01

Young shoots can be hit by aphids, and scale insects turn up on hollies too — check shoot tips and leaf undersides.

## Wiring — brittle branches
- id: il6
- category: wire
- start: 06-15
- end: 08-15

Holly branches are brittle and snap easily, so wire gently and support sharp bends. Summer, with the wood pliable, is the easiest window.

## Semi-ripe cuttings
- id: il7
- category: propagate
- start: 08-15
- end: 09-30

Take semi-ripe cuttings in late summer or early autumn. Seed is possible too but sown outdoors in autumn it can take 2–3 years to germinate.

## Berry display & winter protection
- id: il8
- category: other
- start: 10-15
- end: 12-20

Bright red berries hold on the bare twigs into winter — the whole point of the species. Fully deciduous and genuinely hardy in the ground, but a pot is another matter: give shelter once frosts drop below −5°C.

## Full sun to part shade
- id: il9
- category: other
- start: 05-01
- end: 09-15

Give it full sun or at most part shade — the three sources that actually cover this species agree on that, and good light is what sets a heavy crop of berries. The deeper shade tolerance quoted in bonsai holly guides belongs to the evergreen I. crenata, not here. It's a wetland plant by origin, so keep the soil moist and acidic and never let it dry out fully; unlike most of the bench, it won't punish you for staying wet.
