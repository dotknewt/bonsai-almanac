## Out of winter protection & spring position
- id: ia1
- category: other
- start: 04-15
- end: 05-10

An evergreen broadleaf, so unlike the deciduous hollies it holds its leaves all winter — bring it out of the cold frame or sheltered spot once hard frosts have passed, going by the weather rather than the date. Give sun or light shade: Norway's weaker light favours more sun for dense, well-berried growth, but shelter it from scorching midday sun and cold drying winds, both of which brown the glossy leaves. Keep the soil consistently moist through the growing season and never let it dry out fully.

## Repot as buds extend (every 2–3 years)
- id: ia2
- category: repot
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-15

Bonsai4me repots hollies in spring as the new buds extend, in a basic free-draining mix; aim for a slightly acidic pH of 5–6 and never above 7. Every 2–3 years suits established trees — the annual cadence Bonsai4me gives is for the Japanese hollies its guide actually covers, and PFAF, writing about this species, warns it resents root disturbance and increasingly so with age. So root-prune conservatively, keep it slightly root-bound rather than over-potting, and stretch the interval further on an old tree.

## Spring hard & structural pruning
- id: ia3
- category: prune
- start: 05-01
- end: 06-05

Do any hard or structural cuts as growth resumes in spring, so the slow-healing wounds have the whole season to callus. Work with holly's habit: the very spiny juvenile leaves and coarse young growth give way to smaller, smoother adult leaves on shaded, protected branches, so build the canopy from those where you can.

## Start fortnightly high-nitrogen feed
- id: ia4
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 07-15

Feed every two weeks through the growing season. A nitrogen-rich feed from spring into midsummer builds strong new growth on this naturally slow grower.

## Pest watch — aphids, scale & leaf miner
- id: ia5
- category: pest
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

Young shoots can be hit by aphids, and scale insects turn up on hollies too — check shoot tips and leaf undersides. Watch also for holly leaf miner, whose larvae tunnel pale yellow-brown blotches through the leaves; pick off and destroy mined leaves as they appear.

## Flowering — keep a male nearby for berries
- id: ia6
- category: other
- start: 06-01
- end: 06-30

Holly is dioecious: only a pollinated female plant carries the ornamental red berries, so a male must flower near a female. The small, dull-white, faintly fragrant flowers open in early summer — a little later here than in the UK — so keep both sexes together while pollinators are active.

## Clip-and-grow through summer
- id: ia7
- category: prune
- start: 06-05
- end: 08-31

Once new shoots have extended a few centimetres, trim them back to a couple of leaves to build shape and density; pruning later in the season gives more flower. To keep the autumn–winter berry display on a female, avoid cutting away this year's fruiting wood.

## Wiring — brittle branches
- id: ia8
- category: wire
- start: 06-15
- end: 08-31

Holly branches are brittle and snap easily, so wire gently and support any sharp bend. The bark marks readily — pad the wire and check often as summer growth thickens the branch. Summer, with the wood at its most pliable, is the easiest window; mind the spines while you work.

## Switch to lower-nitrogen late-summer feed
- id: ia9
- category: feed
- start: 07-15
- end: 09-15

From midsummer, swap to a lower-nitrogen feed, still fortnightly, to harden the season's growth before winter. Stop feeding once growth slows in autumn.

## Semi-ripe cuttings
- id: ia10
- category: propagate
- start: 08-15
- end: 09-30

Take semi-ripe cuttings of the current year's green wood in late summer or early autumn — the most reliable way to propagate this evergreen holly.

## Sow seed outdoors
- id: ia11
- category: seed
- start: 09-15
- end: 10-31

Collect ripe berries and sow the seed outdoors in autumn for natural stratification, but be patient: holly seed is slow and erratic and can take 2–3 years to germinate. Cuttings get you a usable tree far faster.

## Berry display & winter protection
- id: ia12
- category: other
- start: 10-20
- end: 12-20

On a pollinated female the red berries ripen through autumn and hold on the evergreen foliage into winter — the classic holly display. The hardiness ratings flatter it: RHS H6 (−15 to −20°C) and PFAF's −15°C are for a tree in the ground, and NIBIO is blunter about what matters here — kristtorn does poorly where mean temperatures fall below −0.5°C, which is why its Norwegian range hugs the mild south and west coast. Inland, treat it as a tree at its climatic edge. Move it into a cold frame, unheated greenhouse or sheltered, mulched spot before hard frosts, taking −5°C as the trigger (bonsai4me's figure for potted hollies). PFAF also notes severe frost can kill whole branches left open to the sky. Being evergreen it keeps transpiring, so shield the leaves from desiccating winter wind and don't let a frozen pot dry out.
