## Out of winter protection
- id: bx1
- category: other
- start: 04-15
- end: 05-10

Bring it out of the cold frame or sheltered spot once hard frosts have passed — go by the weather, not the date. Foliage bronzed or yellowed by winter frost is normal and greens up again as growth resumes.

## Air-layer & collect hedge material
- id: bx2
- category: propagate
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-20

Start air-layers as growth begins, and collect old hedge or garden material bare-rooted now too — both work best as spring growth gets going.

## Full light, generous summer water
- id: bx3
- category: other
- start: 05-01
- end: 09-15

Give plenty of direct light to keep growth strong and foliage dense — Norway's weaker sunlight makes the case for more sun, not the dappled shade sometimes recommended for milder climates. Shade only against scorch at the height of summer. Water generously through summer; it shrugs off short dry spells but hates waterlogged soil, so keep the mix free-draining.

## Let the spring flush run free
- id: bx4
- category: prune
- start: 05-01
- end: 06-05

Don't cut anything yet. Bonsai4me is explicit that box should grow freely through spring to build vigour, and trimming into the first flush costs the tree exactly the energy it's trying to bank. Shaping starts once this flush has fully extended.

## Fortnightly balanced feed
- id: bx5
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 09-20

Feed every two weeks right through the growing season with a balanced or slightly low-nitrogen organic feed to keep leaves small and dense. Stop over winter dormancy, and skip feeding for about four weeks after the midsummer repot while roots recover.

## Wire young, flexible shoots
- id: bx6
- category: wire
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

Boxwood wood is extremely hard and brittle once mature, so wire young shoots rather than forcing bends into old wood. Use aluminium wire and protect the thin, pale bark with raffia — marks and bites show for years. Wire can stay on through the whole slow growing season, but check weekly: young shoots may set in 2–3 weeks, older branches can take up to six months.

## Box tree moth watch
- id: bx7
- category: pest
- start: 05-25
- end: 10-15

Inspect the inner foliage for webbing and green-and-black caterpillars. The box tree moth (buksbommøll) has confirmed Norwegian specimens from 2022–2023 along the southern and western coast, though it isn't yet known to be widely established. Two generations are typical, peaking now and again in August — remove caterpillars by hand or spray Bacillus thuringiensis (Btk) into the webbing in the evening.

## Spider mite & box blight watch
- id: bx8
- category: pest
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-31

Watch for red spider mite in hot, dry weather, and for box blight (buksbomvisnesjuke) — a fungal disease present in Norway since 2010 that causes whole leaves to wither and go grey, dark streaks along the stems, and rapid dieback. It thrives in mild, damp spells, and a dense box canopy makes exactly the shaded, sheltered, humid pocket it wants. Chemical control isn't an option in a private garden, so: cut out and burn infected material at once, disinfect tools between plants with a 1:9 household bleach solution, avoid handling the tree in mild humid weather when spores are flying, water at the soil rather than over the foliage, and isolate it. No resistant varieties exist yet.

## Clip-and-grow & thin the canopy
- id: bx9
- category: prune
- start: 06-05
- end: 09-15

Trim new shoots back to one or two leaf pairs for the rest of the season, and thin the dense outer foliage so light reaches the inner branches and prompts back-budding. Pick off the round summer flower buds too — left on, they drain energy the tree could put into growth.

## Midsummer repot (every 2–3 years)
- id: bx10
- category: repot
- start: 07-05
- end: 07-20

Box responds better to a midsummer repot than the spring timing used for most other bonsai — do it now, in a basic free-draining mix, removing no more than about a third of the roots and keeping the tree slightly root-bound rather than over-potting. A spring repot at bud-swell (roughly late April–May here) also works if you'd rather do it then, but midsummer is the stronger recommendation for this species specifically.

## Semi-ripe cuttings
- id: bx11
- category: propagate
- start: 07-10
- end: 08-20

Take semi-ripe cuttings of 10cm or more now, in summer — both Bonsai4me and the RHS put the main window here. Hardwood cuttings from autumn through to early spring are the other reliable option, giving a near year-round window. Worth knowing: PFAF rates short side shoots taken with a heel in September as the highest-percentage method of all, just slow to root, so a September batch is a good hedge against a poor summer strike.

## Seed (rarely used)
- id: bx12
- category: seed
- start: 09-15
- end: 10-15

Seed is rarely practical for boxwood bonsai — slow and erratic, taking several years just to reach a size worth pruning. If you do try it, sow fresh as soon as it's ripe: box seed needs no cold stratification and germinates in one to three months at around 15°C, so a warm windowsill beats a winter outside. Keep seedlings under cover for their first winter. Cuttings or collected hedge material will still get you a usable tree far faster.

## Move into winter protection
- id: bx13
- category: other
- start: 10-20
- end: 11-20

Move into a cold frame, unheated greenhouse or sheltered, mulched spot before hard frosts set in — typically late October–November in Norway, earlier inland. Treat −4 to −5°C as the trigger for protection (bonsai4me's figure for potted trees), not the RHS's H6 rating (−20 to −15°C): that's for the garden species in the ground, and the RHS itself warns container-grown plants need protection the in-ground rating doesn't cover. Go by the forecast, not the calendar.
