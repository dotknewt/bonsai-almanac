## Out to full sun & heat
- id: c1
- category: other
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

A Mediterranean evergreen that loves heat and full sun. Once nights are reliably frost-free, give it the sunniest, warmest spot you have outdoors for the summer.

## Repot & root-prune in warm spring
- id: c2
- category: repot
- start: 05-20
- end: 06-10

Repot and root-prune together once the days are genuinely warm — carob resents cold, wet roots. Use a gritty, sharply draining mix; it is drought-adapted and rots in soggy soil.

## Water sparingly, let it dry
- id: c3
- category: other
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

Xerophytic and drought-tolerant: let the soil approach dryness between waterings rather than keeping it constantly moist.

## Feed through the growing season
- id: c4
- category: feed
- start: 05-20
- end: 08-31

Feed during active growth. Carob is slow in both trunk and branch, so steady feeding through the warm months helps it build.

## Prune with the repot
- id: c5
- category: prune
- start: 05-20
- end: 06-30

Branch pruning is best done alongside repotting in early summer. Growth is slow, so prune conservatively and let shoots extend before cutting back.

## Pest watch
- id: c6
- category: pest
- start: 06-01
- end: 09-01

Outdoors and in the ground this is a notably clean species — RHS lists it as generally both pest- and disease-free. The risks are the ones that come with indoor overwintering: in still, warm air under glass, watch for scale insects and mealybug on stems and in leaf axils.

## Bring in before frost
- id: c7
- category: other
- start: 09-20
- end: 10-20

Carob is frost tender — RHS rates it H3, surviving only about −5 to 1°C, and even then only in a sheltered spot. In Norway it cannot overwinter outdoors under any circumstances: move it into a frost-free greenhouse or bright cool room before the first frosts and keep it just ticking over until spring. Young spring growth is the most frost-sensitive part of all, so don't rush it back out.

## Wire slowly — slow wood, slow set
- id: c8
- category: wire
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-31

Wire during the warm months while the tree is actually in growth, and work on shoots that have firmed rather than soft new extensions. Growth is slow enough that wire takes a long time to set and equally long to bite, so leave it on patiently but check every few weeks. Protect the bark and make bends gradually over several sessions rather than forcing them.

## Spring flowering
- id: c9
- category: other
- start: 05-01
- end: 06-15

Tiny green-tinted red flowers in racemes 2–6 cm long. RHS puts flowering in spring; PFAF contradicts itself on the same page, saying both spring and August–October, so spring is followed here. In practice a potted carob overwintered indoors this far north is unlikely to flower at all, and the edible pods — which take a full year to ripen — are effectively out of reach. Treat any bloom as a bonus.
