## Full-sun position for the season
- id: m1
- category: other
- start: 04-20
- end: 09-30

Give maximum light from snowmelt onwards — insufficient sunlight means extended needles and dieback of shaded branches. Plenty of water too, but the soil must drain fast; roots should never sit in water. If you're buying material, look for ordinary landscape mugos rather than dwarf rock-garden cultivars like 'Mops' or 'Valley Cushion', which are weak and unresponsive as bonsai.

## Start strong slow-release feeding
- id: m2
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 08-25

Feed strongly with a slow-release fertiliser from spring through to late summer — mugos take heavy feeding and repay it with vigour.

## Pinch the strongest candles
- id: m3
- category: prune
- start: 05-25
- end: 06-20

When candles reach 2.5–4 cm, gently break away the top of any that are noticeably more vigorous than the rest to balance energy across the tree.

## Bud selection — reduce to pairs
- id: m4
- category: prune
- start: 06-05
- end: 07-31

Wherever buds cluster, reduce to 2 so each branch forks into two sub-branches. Remove excess buds as and when they appear through spring and summer.

## Pest & needle-cast check
- id: m5
- category: pest
- start: 06-01
- end: 09-15

Watch for aphids, adelgids and pine sawfly larvae on new growth, and for pine shoot moth. Needle discolouration in spring or summer can mean fungal needle cast — autumn yellowing of the oldest needles is just natural shedding.

## Cut back the first flush
- id: m6
- category: prune
- start: 07-01
- end: 07-20

Cut the first flush back to 1–4 sets of new needles (by branch strength) to force a compact second flush this season. Never remove more than 50% of top growth in one year, and keep old needles wherever you want backbuds — mugos backbud where needles remain, not where they've been removed.

## Repot window — summer, not spring
- id: m7
- category: repot
- start: 07-20
- end: 08-20

Mugos react far better to summer repotting than the spring timing used for other pines: do it after the first flush is cut back, which lands in July–August. Unusually for a pine, this species transplants well. Pick old, poor-draining soil out by hand (never wash the roots); on weaker trees remove only half the compacted soil. In heat above ~27°C, shade for two weeks and mist the foliage twice daily.

## Final feed of the season
- id: m8
- category: feed
- start: 08-25
- end: 09-10

Feeding runs to late summer, then stops so the second flush can harden off before the Norwegian winter. Bonsai Empire runs one-flush pines all the way to late autumn, but that suits milder climates — this far north, stopping now is the safer call.

## Styling & wiring window
- id: m9
- category: wire
- start: 09-01
- end: 10-15

Late summer/early autumn is the main window to style and wire mugo pines. One insult per year: after repotting, styling or drastic pruning, wait 12 months before the next major work — a tree styled now can't be repotted next summer.

## Heavy pruning / trunk chops
- id: m10
- category: prune
- start: 10-01
- end: 11-15

Autumn is the time for trunk chops and heavy branch removal on pines. Leave a short stump and seal the wound. This counts as the year's one insult.

## Winter — hardiness check
- id: m11
- category: other
- start: 10-20
- end: 03-15

A mountain native, mugo shrugs off severe cold and tolerates strong wind including maritime exposure, but shield it from freezing winds while the rootball is frozen — a pot gives roots none of the insulation the ground does. Yellowing oldest needles now is natural shedding, not trouble.

## Pine rust watch — keep peonies apart
- id: m12
- category: pest
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-31

NIBIO records mugo acting as an alternate host for pionfiltrust (Cronartium flaccidum) in Norway. The fungus can only complete its life cycle by alternating between pine and peony, so the simple defence is not to stand the tree near Paeonia. Look for swollen, blistered bark oozing orange-yellow spores on stems and branches.

## Seed & grafting
- id: m13
- category: seed
- start: 09-15
- end: 10-31

Sow seed fresh in individual pots in a cold frame as soon as it's ripe; stored seed wants about 6 weeks of cold stratification at 4°C before a late-winter sowing. Named forms are grafted rather than grown from seed.
