## Bright, warm & humid through winter
- id: fi1
- category: other
- start: 11-15
- end: 03-15

Keep above 15°C, on the brightest windowsill available, and clear of cold draughts — never place above a radiator. A gravel tray under the pot helps humidity through the dry, centrally-heated months; the thick waxy leaves let it survive poor conditions, but growth stays weak until light and humidity improve.

## Winter pest check
- id: fi2
- category: pest
- start: 01-10
- end: 03-01

Scale insects and spider mites are the main problems, especially in dry, heated indoor air — check stem joints and leaf undersides. Ficus won't tolerate insecticides containing Malathion.

## Repot (every 2 years)
- id: fi3
- category: repot
- start: 04-15
- end: 05-15

Repot in spring into a good-quality, well-aerated soil mix. Imported trees often arrive in poor, congealed compost — replace it at the first opportunity rather than waiting for a second repot.

## Start fortnightly feeding
- id: fi4
- category: feed
- start: 04-20
- end: 09-15

Feed fortnightly with a high-nitrogen or balanced fertiliser as soon as active growth is visible. Stop again the moment growth stalls or the tree looks unwell — don't feed a resting or stressed tree.

## Hard pruning window
- id: fi5
- category: prune
- start: 04-15
- end: 06-01

Spring is the ideal time for hard pruning, though figs tolerate cuts year-round. They bleed a milky white latex when cut, and that sap is itself the wound seal — let it well up and dry rather than wiping it away, though cut paste is worth using on the largest wounds. On smaller branches, leave a short stub to dry out and remove cleanly a few days later. Always leave a bud or leaf at a branch tip — a bare tip dies back.

## Watering through active growth
- id: fi6
- category: other
- start: 04-15
- end: 09-30

Keep the soil evenly moist while the tree is growing, watering again as the surface starts to dry — don't let it dry out fully. Overwatering and poor placement are the two most common causes of leaf drop; a stressed tree usually leafs out again within 3–6 weeks once conditions improve.

## Wiring — check often as it thickens
- id: fi7
- category: wire
- start: 05-01
- end: 08-31

Wire once shoots have firmed slightly — very soft new growth tears and marks easily. Branches thicken fast in the growing season, so check every few weeks and remove wire before it bites into the soft bark.

## Take cuttings or air-layer
- id: fi8
- category: propagate
- start: 05-01
- end: 08-15

Figs root very easily from cuttings taken at any time of year, and also take well to air-layering. The warmth of the growing season gives the fastest, most reliable results.

## Optional outdoor spell in settled warmth
- id: fi9
- category: other
- start: 06-20
- end: 08-10

Move outdoors only once night temperatures are reliably staying above 15°C — both bonsai sources treat that as the floor, not a target, and figs want a steady temperature rather than a swinging one. In Norway that window is short and unreliable, so leaving it on a bright indoor sill year-round is often the safer choice. If moved out, pick a sheltered spot and bring it straight back in at the first cool night.

## Bring indoors before autumn cools
- id: fi10
- category: other
- start: 08-20
- end: 09-10

If it spent summer outside, bring it back in well before night temperatures drop toward 15°C, and reacclimatise it gradually to indoor light to limit stress-related leaf drop.

## Taper feeding — or feed on through
- id: fi11
- category: feed
- start: 09-10
- end: 10-10

What happens next depends on your light. Under a grow light the tree often keeps growing straight through the dark months: drop from fortnightly to a monthly feed rather than stopping, since a tree still in growth still needs feeding. On an unlit windowsill through a Norwegian winter it will effectively rest, and then you stop — feeding a resting tree only buys weak, leggy growth that won't harden off.

## Autumn/winter pest recheck
- id: fi12
- category: pest
- start: 09-15
- end: 11-01

Check again for scale and spider mites as the tree resettles indoors for the dark months, when dry heated air favours both.

## Optional summer leaf-thinning (vigorous trees only)
- id: fi13
- category: prune
- start: 06-15
- end: 08-01

F. microcarpa leaves shrink on their own with normal cultivation and pruning, so hard full defoliation isn't worth the risk here — the guides that promote a fixed June defoliation assume a dormant outdoor tree, which this isn't. Only if a tree is genuinely vigorous and in strong light (a bright summer sill, ideally with a grow light) can you thin oversized leaves selectively to push finer ramification and smaller regrowth: snip through the leaf stalk and always leave a leaf or bud at each branch tip, because a Ficus branch stripped bare dies back fast. Skip it entirely on a weak, recently-repotted, or dimly-lit tree, and never strip the whole canopy at once.
