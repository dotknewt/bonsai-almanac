## Repot before bud break
- id: nm1
- category: repot
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-10

Repot in a free-draining mix (e.g. 2 akadama : 1 bark : 1 pumice) before buds swell, with up to a third root reduction on established trees. Unlike top growth, roots don't bleed when cut, so this is a good window to combine repotting with a structural cut. Go by bud movement, not the date.

## Biweekly balanced feed
- id: nm2
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 08-20

Feed every two weeks from spring leaf-out to late summer with a balanced, low-nitrogen fertiliser. Ease off early feed on refined trees to keep growth fine — this is a vigorous species that coarsens fast if pushed.

## Dormant structural pruning
- id: nm3
- category: prune
- start: 03-01
- end: 04-05

Make large structural cuts while fully dormant, before sap starts moving — Norway maple bleeds heavily from top cuts made once growth begins. Late summer is the other safe window if you miss this one; avoid late spring/early summer entirely.

## Cutback to two nodes
- id: nm4
- category: prune
- start: 06-01
- end: 07-10

Once a shoot has made 5–6 nodes, cut back to the last two to build ramification. Pinch the apex on mature trees to encourage lower, finer branching — it buds back willingly.

## Leaf-cut for size reduction
- id: nm5
- category: prune
- start: 07-05
- end: 08-05

Leaf-cutting now prompts fresh budding and modestly smaller leaves, but expect only around 50% reduction — this species suits a medium-to-large bonsai rather than fine refinement. Only do this on strong, healthy trees.

## Wire young shoots
- id: nm6
- category: wire
- start: 05-20
- end: 07-15

Wire while shoots are still soft and green — the wood stiffens and gets brittle fast. Check often, since vigorous growth thickens branches quickly and wire can bite in within weeks.

## Watch for verticillium & aphids
- id: nm7
- category: pest
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

Verticillium wilt is the serious risk here — soil-borne, often fatal, and untreatable, so disinfect tools between trees. Otherwise watch for aphids on foliage and mild anthracnose after a cool wet spring, which the tree usually shrugs off.

## Air-layer or take cuttings
- id: nm8
- category: propagate
- start: 06-01
- end: 06-30

Air-layer as spring growth hardens, or take cuttings — both work reasonably well on this species. It also self-seeds prolifically if you'd rather grow fresh material from seed.

## Collect & sow seed outdoors
- id: nm9
- category: seed
- start: 09-15
- end: 10-31

Collect seed in autumn and sow outdoors to stratify naturally over winter. Seed needs an obligate 90–120 days of cold, moist stratification around 3–4°C before it will germinate.

## Cold-stratify indoors if unsown
- id: nm10
- category: seed
- start: 11-15
- end: 12-31

If seed wasn't sown outdoors in autumn, start cold-moist stratification indoors (around 3–4°C) now for a spring sowing — it needs the full 90–120 days before it will break dormancy.

## Summer positioning & watering
- id: nm11
- category: other
- start: 05-01
- end: 09-15

Give morning or evening sun but shade at the height of summer — the foliage scorches more easily than it looks. Keep soil moist but not wet; this species is prone to root rot in poorly-drained mix.

## Winter root protection
- id: nm12
- category: other
- start: 11-01
- end: 03-01

Hardy as a landscape tree, but not the Arctic species it's sometimes made out to be: NIBIO calls spisslønn varmekjært — heat-loving — with its Norwegian range concentrated in Østlandet and Sørlandet and its northward spread checked around Dovrefjell. A potted rootball freezes far harder than ground soil in any case, so mulch or shelter the pot through the coldest stretch of winter.
