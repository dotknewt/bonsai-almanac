## Sunny spot, semi-shade in real heat
- id: d1
- category: other
- start: 04-20
- end: 09-30

Stand it in full sun — this species cannot cope with shade — but give it as much side shelter as you can, and move it to semi-shade through the hottest weeks of summer. A fast-growing swamp tree, it is one of the few bonsai happy staying wet: keep the soil moist through summer and never let the pot dry out. It still resents standing in stagnant water, so the mix must drain even while holding moisture.

## Repot (every 2 years) as buds move
- id: d2
- category: repot
- start: 04-15
- end: 05-10

Being deciduous, it repots in spring as buds move, and it takes considerable root pruning without complaint — about a third of the rootmass is safe. Every second year suits most trees; use a mix that holds moisture but still drains.

## High-nitrogen in spring, cautious after
- id: d3
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 08-25

Use high-nitrogen fertiliser in spring only. Ease right off through summer — this tree is vigorous enough on its own, and heavy summer feeding buys uncontrollable growth and long internodes rather than ramification.

## Bud removal & spring twig pruning
- id: d4
- category: prune
- start: 05-20
- end: 08-31

Redwoods throw a lot of new buds, especially around old pruning scars — rub off the ones you don't want as soon as you see them, or they thicken into clutter. Twigs with long internodes are best shortened in spring before the new buds open. Because buds are opposite, remove one of each pair at forks to avoid swollen bar branches.

## Wire with care — brittle branches
- id: d5
- category: wire
- start: 05-20
- end: 07-15

Mostly a formal-upright, clip-and-grow species. Branches can be wired but are quite brittle, so for lowering a branch a guy wire is usually the better tool than coiled wire. New shoots take wire while still soft, but they lignify and thicken fast — watch closely for biting in.

## Pest watch — aphids
- id: d6
- category: pest
- start: 05-15
- end: 08-31

About as trouble-free as bonsai get; aphids on the soft new growth are the main thing to watch for.

## Autumn colour
- id: d7
- category: other
- start: 10-01
- end: 10-31

The soft foliage turns tawny orange-brown to red-brown before dropping — a deciduous conifer, so expect it to go bare for winter.

## Protect roots from hard freeze
- id: d8
- category: other
- start: 11-15
- end: 03-01

Needs its winter dormancy, and in the ground it is hardy to around −30°C — but a container is the exception the bonsai sources single out, and the shallow, moisture-loving roots are damaged by a hard freeze in a pot. Shelter it in a cold frame or heel the pot into the ground through deep Norwegian cold. Watch spring as well as midwinter: fresh growth is vulnerable to late frosts.
