Care windows from the Bonsai4me Acer palmatum guide (a true species-level guide), cross-checked against Bonsai Empire, RHS, PFAF and Missouri Botanical Garden, shifted ~3-4 weeks for Norway. On sun exposure the sources genuinely split — Bonsai4me (UK) says keep it out of direct sun year-round, while Missouri BG notes full sun is fine "in the northern parts of its growing range"; the northern reading is followed here, as in the Boxwood and Holly entries. Summer hard-pruning follows the two bonsai sources over Missouri BG's garden-tree advice to avoid it.

## Good light, shelter from wind
- id: a1
- category: other
- start: 04-15
- end: 09-30

Give it real direct light through a Norwegian summer — sun this far north is weak enough that the scorch warnings written for the UK and the American Midwest rarely bite, and light is what drives vigour and autumn colour. Shade only against genuine heat, from about 30°C. Strong wind is the bigger risk here: the thin leaves burn at the edges. Green-leaved varieties are tougher than red.

## Repot as buds extend (every 2–5 yrs)
- id: a2
- category: repot
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-10

Repot just as buds start to move in spring, then stop once shoots extend. Early-leafing varieties like Kiyohime can go a little later. Free-draining soil.

## Spring feed — by tree stage
- id: a3
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 06-15

Split this by what the tree needs. While trunk or branch structure is still developing, feed weekly with high-nitrogen fertiliser as soon as leafbuds open — Bonsai4me reckons it also toughens leaves against sun and wind scorch. On a refined tree, hold the nitrogen back and use a balanced feed instead: Bonsai Empire warns high nitrogen buys you oversized leaves and long internodes, which is exactly what refinement is trying to undo.

## Pinch new shoots to shape
- id: a4
- category: prune
- start: 05-20
- end: 07-15

Pinch or cut new growth back to one or two pairs of leaves, leaving two opposing leaves, to build fine twiggy ramification. Never do structural pruning now.

## Switch to balanced feed
- id: a5
- category: feed
- start: 06-15
- end: 08-25

Once spring growth hardens off, feed fortnightly with a balanced fertiliser through the rest of the season.

## Pest & disease watch
- id: a6
- category: pest
- start: 05-15
- end: 09-15

Watch for aphids, scale insects and caterpillars, plus fungal problems like verticillium wilt. Keep airflow good to limit mildew on shaded inner growth.

## Mid-summer leaf-cut & hard pruning
- id: a7
- category: prune
- start: 07-01
- end: 07-25

The semi-dormant midsummer window is safe for hard/structural pruning and, on healthy trees only, partial leaf-cutting — never in a repotting year or two years running. Seal larger wounds.

## Wiring window
- id: a8
- category: wire
- start: 05-01
- end: 10-20

Wire any time from spring to late autumn. Bare branches in spring, after defoliation in midsummer, or after leaf-fall are easiest. Bark marks readily, so check often.

## Autumn hard-pruning window
- id: a9
- category: prune
- start: 10-15
- end: 11-10

The other safe time for hard and formative pruning is within about a week of leaf-fall. Crucially, never prune in spring — all acers bleed profusely then and can lose branches or be badly weakened.

## Winter dormancy & frost protection
- id: a10
- category: other
- start: 11-15
- end: 03-01

Acers need a real dormancy (below ~10°C for at least 44 days) but the fine roots are frost-tender in a pot — shelter below −10°C. In the ground this is a genuinely hardy tree (RHS rates it H6, −20 to −15°C; PFAF says about −25°C), which is exactly why the pot trigger looks so cautious by comparison: a rootball in a bonsai pot gets none of the insulation ground soil provides. Norwegian winters cover the cold requirement easily.

## Air-layer & take cuttings
- id: a11
- category: propagate
- start: 06-01
- end: 07-31

Air-layering is the main way maples are propagated — start one as soon as this year's spring growth has hardened off, and expect roughly 12 months before you can separate it. Softwood cuttings also strike now: slice a thin sliver of bark from the base and dip in rooting hormone.

## Collect & sow seed
- id: a12
- category: seed
- start: 09-20
- end: 10-31

Sow the winged seed as soon as it's ripe in a cold frame and let the winter do the work. Stored seed needs a 24-hour soak followed by 2–4 months of cold stratification at 1–8°C before it will germinate.
