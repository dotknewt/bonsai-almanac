Bonsai4me's Juniperus guide is written at genus level and never mentions J. procumbens — it is framed around J. x media, with J. chinensis, sargentii, squamata, rigida, communis, sabina and horizontalis also named. Care here is that genus guidance, cross-checked against Bonsai Empire and the RHS entry for J. procumbens 'Nana', shifted ~3-4 weeks for Norway. The RHS rates the cultivar H7 (below −20°C) in the ground; that is an in-ground figure and the −10°C pot trigger below is what actually applies to a bonsai.

## Spring health check
- id: j1
- category: other
- start: 04-20
- end: 05-10

Junipers wake late, and a dead one can hold normal foliage colour for weeks after the roots have died. Confirm new growth is actually extending before doing any real work. Full sun suits procumbens' needle foliage — it's the scale-leaved junipers that want a little midday shade.

## Start high-nitrogen feeding
- id: j2
- category: feed
- start: 05-10
- end: 07-15

Feed fortnightly with a high-nitrogen fertiliser from the start of growth until midsummer. An occasional acidic feed helps, especially in hard-water areas.

## Pinch & scissor-prune new shoots
- id: j3
- category: prune
- start: 06-01
- end: 09-15

Through the growing season, pinch only soft new tips; scissor-cut anything that has hardened — pinching hardened growth tears the bark away. Never shear the whole canopy like a hedge: taking every growing tip at once weakens the tree. Always leave a branch enough foliage to support itself, or it will die back.

## Wiring — growth is supple
- id: j4
- category: wire
- start: 05-01
- end: 09-30

Juniper wood is extremely flexible — even branches several centimetres thick stay supple enough to shape, and they take aggressive bending if you wrap them in raffia or tape first. Wire can go on year-round, but it takes 3 months to a year to set, so check often for biting as branches thicken.

## Pest watch
- id: j5
- category: pest
- start: 06-01
- end: 09-15

Dense pads shelter spider mites and scale. Part the foliage and check the interior — mite damage shows as dull, grey-green needles. Juniper aphids, needle miners and webworms turn up too, and a healthy, well-placed tree shrugs off most of them.

## Switch to balanced feed
- id: j6
- category: feed
- start: 07-15
- end: 10-15

From midsummer, swap the high-nitrogen fertiliser for a balanced feed, still fortnightly, and continue until winter.

## Thin the foliage pads
- id: j7
- category: prune
- start: 08-01
- end: 09-15

Repeated pruning makes pads dense enough to block light and air from interior and lower branches, weakening them until they die back. Thin now so inner shoots stay strong.

## Repot (every 3–5 years)
- id: j8
- category: repot
- start: 08-20
- end: 09-20

Junipers repot best in late summer/early autumn, not spring — Bonsai4me calls this a much better window than the traditional late-spring timing. An inorganic clay-based soil is essential. Never bare-root — replace no more than a third of the soil in any one repotting. Once established in good soil, every 3–5 years is enough.

## Autumn watering check
- id: j9
- category: other
- start: 09-15
- end: 10-31

Junipers in old, compacted organic soil rot easily if overwatered. As growth slows and autumn rain sets in, make sure the pot drains freely and ease off the can — let the surface dry slightly before watering again.

## Structural wiring window
- id: j10
- category: wire
- start: 10-01
- end: 11-15

The whole tree will need 100% wiring at least once in its life, and autumn is a calm window for it. No heavy bends in trunks or branches once temperatures reach 0°C or below.

## Winter protection below −10°C
- id: j11
- category: other
- start: 11-01
- end: 03-15

Fully hardy outdoors, but shelter it (cold frame or similar) when frosts drop below −10°C. Don't stash a conifer in a dark outbuilding unless temperatures are consistently below −10°C — and never overwinter it indoors, where low light, dry air and lost dormancy will kill it.

## Seeds & cuttings
- id: j12
- category: propagate
- start: 06-01
- end: 08-31

Junipers propagate from seed or cuttings, though most people start from nursery stock — garden-centre junipers in a range of sizes are some of the best cheap bonsai material there is.
